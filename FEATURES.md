# Features

What the app does now. Edit in place when a change alters what users see or can do.

## What it does

- `offer-engine check-env` lists each `.env` key as `set` or `missing`, never its value. It exits 1 when `OFFER_BOT_TOKEN`, `OFFER_BOT_USERNAME` or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is missing or `CUSTOMER_BOTS` is malformed.
- `offer-engine setup` walks a developer through five steps: create the Offer Bot in BotFather, turn on Bot-to-Bot Communication Mode, create 6–8 Customer Bots, enter the Anthropic API key and model IDs, then check `.env`. It checks each bot token with Telegram `getMe`, reads tokens and the key hidden, never prints them, and saves `.env` with mode 0600 after each step. Running it again offers to keep each value already set.
- `offer-engine db init` creates the SQLite database at `DB_PATH` (default `data/offer_engine.db`) or applies new schema files. Running it again is safe.
- `offer-engine clock show` prints the simulated time, which starts at `2026-12-01 00:00`. `offer-engine clock advance --hours N` or `--days N [--seed S]` runs the marketplace forward one hour at a time and never moves back. Every generated Customer visits, views, carts, abandons, orders, gets deliveries and returns, and earns, burns or loses points, and the `customers` table's points follow. Orders placed in the last days of the history arrive on time. Each hour sent Offers resolve by Ground Truth. A Customer without a chat buys with the Offer's 7-day chance at a time before expiry, claiming first; the `order` (basket before discount) and `offer_redeemed` (the Offer's cost) events carry the Offer's ID, and the delivery follows 3 days later. A quarter of non-buyers claim without buying, about 5% dismiss and 1% opt out (Stop). A Tester who claims buys with chance p / (p + 0.25 · (1 − p)), and the Offer Bot then sends "Simulated order: ₹1,240 basket, code POE-… used. You saved ₹186." A Customer Bot's Offer resolves by its role-play (below). Unredeemed Offers become `expired` at `expires_at`. A Customer with an Offer places no organic orders from the send until the Redemption, or for 7 days if they don't buy. Queued Offers to Customers without a chat become `sent` at their send time. Every hour four Triggers run the decision pipeline: an abandoned cart, points expiring within `points_expiry_warning_days` (7), `long_gap_days` (45) since the last order, and a Customer's first delivery. Each underlying event (the cart, the expiry date, the last order, the first delivery) fires once, and the Rules still decide whether an Offer goes out. At 10:00 each simulated day the Daily Sweep runs the pipeline for every Customer without a Decision in the last 24 simulated hours. It decides chat-attached Customers first, so the weekly budget reaches them before anyone else, and logs No Offer Decisions too. The command prints the new time and the counts (`sessions 2,605 · product_views 5,243 · … · orders 46 · … · offers_sent 3,176 · claimed 804 · redeemed 92 · dismissed 133 · opted_out 25 · expired 1,503 · sweep.offer 8,357 · sweep.no_offer 1,506 · sweep.skipped 137 · decisions.cart_abandoned 150 · … · sweep.binding.holdout_pct 975`). The same seed and starting state give the same events, and stopping mid-day changes nothing.
- Rule settings are versioned in the database. Version 0 is the defaults, and a revert saves a new version equal to the old one. The console's Rules page shows every Rule as a plain-English sentence with a blank to fill ("Never discount below __% margin", "Send at most __ offers per 7 days", "Give gold Customers at most __% off"), grouped as Discounts, Frequency, Budget, Timing, Fatigue and Holdout. A per-category or per-tier cap left blank means no cap. **Save** needs **Your name** and a **Note**, refuses a send window that doesn't start before it ends, and saves nothing when nothing changed. The version history lists each version with who, simulated and wall-clock time, note and the fields it changed, down to version 0. **Revert to this version** on any older row saves it again as a new version, under the typed name. A change applies to new Decisions only. Offers keep the rule version they passed. The budget mode radio switches the Reserved Budget between worst-case cost (default) and expected cost. Expected shows "Expected-cost budgeting can overspend if more Offers are redeemed than predicted." and this week's worst-case exposure against the budget. Changing the Holdout percentage shows a warning that it changes who is in the Holdout. **Preview impact** runs the Daily Sweep at the next simulated 10:00 twice on copies of the database, once under the saved Rules and once under the form's edits, with the rules chooser deciding for everyone. It shows the difference as `-837 Offers · -₹1,770 worst-case cost · -₹247 expected cost`, how many Customers lose or gain an Offer, and the change in Offers by Offer Type and in Decisions by Binding Rule. It saves nothing, and with no edits it says so. On 10k Customers it takes 50–70 s.
- The Rules page also has **Withdraw outstanding Offers**, enabled only after ticking the confirmation box. It marks every unexpired `queued`, `sent` or `claimed` live Offer `withdrawn`, and the Offer Bot tells each chat that has seen one "This offer has been withdrawn: POE-…. Sorry for the change." Queued Offers get no notice, since their card never went out. The page logs each withdraw with who, when and how many Offers.
- `offer-engine start` runs the Offer Bot, the Customer Bot simulator and the console together, prefixing each line of output with the process name. Ctrl-C stops all three. If one exits with an error, the others stop.
- `make` runs the project in one command. It installs dependencies, exits if `.env` lacks a required key, builds the 10k seed-7 world if the database is missing (`generate`, `lift fit`, then `chats link-showcase` for the frozen Persona Cards) and runs `offer-engine start`. An existing database is kept. `make reset` deletes it and builds a new world, `make console` runs only the console and `make test` runs the tests. `make demo-snapshot` and `make demo-reset` run `offer-engine demo snapshot` and `demo reset`. Set `DB=` if `.env` sets `DB_PATH`.
- The marketing console (`uv run streamlit run console/app.py`, or `offer-engine start`, at http://localhost:8501) shows the simulated time in the sidebar of every page. When `CONSOLE_PASSWORD` is set, it asks for that password once per browser session. The Simulation Clock page has **+1 hour** and **+1 day** buttons and an Auto mode that advances one simulated day every 5–60 wall-clock seconds (slider, default 10). Each runs the same day-step as `clock advance`, with `SEED` from `.env`, and the page then lists what the step did (sessions, orders, Offers sent, Decisions per Trigger). The console runs one step at a time: a press or Auto tick while a step runs does nothing. The Decision log page lists up to 500 Decisions, newest first, with time, Customer, Trigger, Offer terms, chooser, reason, Binding Rule, rule version, Validation result and failures. Filters are Customer ID, Trigger, chooser and Hide No Offer (on by default). Selecting a row shows the Offer's code, status, caption and full reason, and for a Fallback the agent's rejected proposal. Its **Why** expander lists the Decision's trace, one line per stage.
- The console's Results page has five sections, each chart with a one-line caption on what to look for. **Policies** charts Incremental Margin per ₹ of offer spend by Policy from the latest `simulate` run, with a table of Offers, Claims, Redemptions, spend and Incremental Margin, the run's time and the command to rerun it. A radio switches between the 500-Customer sample (default, the only scope with Rules + Agent) and all Customers. **Live funnel vs Holdout** charts live Offers sent, Claimed and Redeemed since the clock start and compares orders per Customer outside and inside the Holdout. **Wasted on Sure Things** shows live spend on Sure Things and each Policy's. **Validation** charts rejections and Fallbacks per simulated day and lists the top failure codes in plain English. **Lift by decile** plots mean Estimated Lift against mean Ground Truth Lift per decile for a chosen Policy. Tester copies are left out of every number.
- `offer-engine chats link CUSTOMER_ID --bot @username` makes a Customer Bot from `CUSTOMER_BOTS` the chat for that Customer. A chat serves one Customer, so linking a bot again moves it. `offer-engine chats link-showcase` links each frozen Persona Card's Customer to a Customer Bot, in case order: the first card gets the first bot. It prints `C09823  cart_abandoner       Kavya Joshi            @ap_customer5_bot` per link. With fewer bots than cards it skips the optional `persuadable` card and says so, and with fewer bots than the other cards it stops with an error. `offer-engine chats list` prints each chat-attached Customer with its kind, bot or chat ID, and whether it opted out.
- Every 5 wall-clock seconds the Offer Bot sends each queued Offer whose Customer is chat-attached and opted in, once its `send_at` has passed on the Simulation Clock and before it expires. The Offer Card is a PNG of the terms (for example "15% off Fashion", "Up to ₹300 off", the code and "Valid until 4 Dec, 10:00"). Its caption is the agent's or the rules chooser's caption, a blank line and `Code: POE-…`, and it has the buttons Claim, Not now and Stop. The Offer becomes `sent` with its simulated `sent_at`. A Customer Bot gets its card by `@username`, the only way Telegram delivers one bot's message to another. The bot also sends any unsent `outbox` messages.
- Claim (button or `/claim CODE`, on any line of a text message) marks the Offer `claimed` and replies "Claimed! Your code is POE-…. It's valid until 4 Dec, 10:00." Not now (button or `/notnow CODE`) marks it `dismissed` and replies "No problem. We'll ease off for a bit."; three of these within 14 simulated days start a Fatigue Pause. Stop (button or `/stop`) opts the Customer out, withdraws their queued Offers and replies "You won't get any more offers. Send /start any time to turn them back on." Each records an event (`offer_claimed`, `offer_dismissed`, `opted_out`) at the clock's time. Only the chat linked to the Offer's Customer may act on it ("That code isn't yours."), and an expired, withdrawn or unknown code gets "That code isn't active any more." A tapped card loses its buttons.
- `offer-engine start` runs every Customer Bot in `CUSTOMER_BOTS` in one process. When a Customer Bot gets an Offer Card for its linked Customer, it plays that Customer. Claude (`FAST_AGENT_MODEL`) reads the Persona Card, the caption and the card's terms, scores the message's fit from −1 to 1, and writes two in-character replies, one for taking the code and one for passing. The chance of buying is Ground Truth's, times 1 + 0.3 × fit, so the agent moves it by at most 30% of itself. A weighted coin flip on that chance decides. A buyer claims, a quarter of the others claim without buying, and the rest say Not now. The bot sends the matching reply with `/claim CODE` or `/notnow CODE` on its own line, for example "Thanks for the reminder, I'm still comparing basmati brands. I'll come back to it later." then `/notnow POE-VYAP91`. A buyer's Redemption comes at a random time before expiry. The bot reacts once per Offer. Without the agent (no key or model, or a failed call) the fit is 0 and the reply is neutral. `sim_bot_responses` records each reaction: the base chance, the fit, the chance used, the outcome and the reply.
- A person who sends `/start` to the Offer Bot gets "Welcome! Pick a Customer to play. You'll get the offers our engine decides for them." with one button per frozen Persona Card whose Customer is in this world (for example "Kavya Joshi · Cart abandoner") and **Surprise me**, which picks one at random. Picking gives the Tester their own copy of that Customer, `<id>-t<Telegram user id>`, made on the first pick and reused after. The copy has the Customer's profile and points, the last 180 days of events, and every past Offer that is finished or expired, with new codes, so the engine decides for it as it would for the original. Its Claims resolve by the original's Ground Truth. The bot replies with the Persona Card and "Simulated date: 3 Dec 2026. The engine reviews Kavya Joshi every simulated day at 10:00, as the marketing team advances the clock. Send /offers to ask for one now." Picking the Holdout Customer replies "You're playing Meenal Tripathi, who is in the control group (Holdout). They never receive offers, so we can measure what offers change." `/offers` runs the decision pipeline at once (Trigger `on_request`) and replies "The engine picked an offer for Kavya Joshi. It's on its way.", or the simulated time it goes out when the send window hasn't opened. A No Offer names its Binding Rule in plain words, for example "No offer right now: Kavya Joshi already got 2 offers this week." `/whoami` names the Customer being played and `/switch` shows the buttons again. Switching opts the old copy out and withdraws its queued Offers, and picking it again opts it back in. While a console step holds the database for over 10 s, `/offers` and picks reply "The marketplace is busy moving the simulated clock. Try again in a few seconds." `/start` also turns Offers back on after Stop. From a Customer Bot, `/start` only turns Offers back on (event `opted_in`).
- `offer-engine generate --customers N --months M --seed S` builds a fictional marketplace: 5 categories (margins 10–45%), 200 products in ₹, N Customers (IDs `C00001…` in signup order) and M months of events ending at the clock start (sessions, product views, carts, abandoned carts, orders, deliveries, returns and points). The history also holds random past Offers, as A/B tests would leave them. Customers outside the Holdout get one at 30% of moments (an abandoned cart, 45 days without an order, a Friday 18:00 broadcast), at most one per 7 days. Its type and depth are random. Ground Truth decides whether the Customer buys while the Offer is valid, and the order it places is linked to the Offer. 25% of the rest Claim without buying, a few dismiss it, and 1% opt out. It prints counts per table and per event kind. The same seed gives the same database. Each run deletes the old world first, including Offers, Decisions and chat links.
- `offer-engine eligible --demo` prints the Eligible Set for a built-in Customer under the default Rules, and `--customer ID` does the same for a generated Customer at the clock's time under the current Rules. It shows each allowed Offer Type with its parameter ranges and the Binding Rule for each limit, the send window, and the options dropped with the Rule that emptied them.
- `offer-engine trigger cart-abandoned CUSTOMER_ID` records an abandoned cart at the clock's time (the Customer's latest carted product, else the latest viewed one) and runs the decision pipeline. A second call for the same Customer at the same simulated time is refused. The Rules give the Eligible Set. For a chat-attached Customer, when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is set, Claude (the model in `OFFER_AGENT_MODEL`) picks one option or No Offer, sets its parameters and featured products, and writes the caption and reason (`chooser=agent`). Every other Customer gets the rules chooser, which picks the option and parameters with the highest Lift × margin − expected cost, or No Offer if none is positive (`chooser=rules`). Both use the Estimated Lift for the Customer's segment once `offer-engine lift fit` has run, else a fixed guess per Offer Type; the reason says `(estimated)` or `(prior)`. Validation re-checks the pick, Caption Check included. If the agent's pick fails, or the API errors or takes over 20 s, the rules chooser decides instead (`chooser=fallback`) and the Decision keeps the agent's raw answer and every failure. The command prints the Decision (`pct_category beauty pct=8 cap=70 · chooser=rules · binding: max_pct_by_loyalty_tier · rule v0 · valid`) and, for an Offer, its `POE-` code, send and expiry times, worst-case and expected cost, caption and reason. A Fallback Decision also shows the agent's rejected proposal and the Validation failures. The Offer is saved as `queued` and counts against the weekly budget at worst-case cost (or expected cost in that budget mode).
- `generate` also gives every Customer a hidden Ground Truth: a buying chance for each Offer Type that rises (or, for Sleeping Dogs, falls) with the Offer's depth, and a Response Class. Only the simulator reads it. `offer-engine ground-truth summary` prints the Response Class shares against calibration target 9, plus the mean base chance and max Lift per Offer Type.
- `offer-engine calibrate` measures the generated history against the calibration targets in `docs/research/ecommerce-benchmarks.md` and prints one row per figure: its target number, value, range and `ok`, `MISS` or `miss (category split)`. It covers conversion, the weekly buying chance, cart abandonment and recovery, Redemption per send, repeat rate, orders per year, lapse, margins, spend concentration, Response Classes, festive share, points breakage and opt-out. It reads only events before the clock start, so it still works after `clock advance`. `--strict` exits 1 when any figure other than a category split misses.
- `offer-engine history summary` prints the past Offers per Offer Type (sent, Claim and Redemption rates, the 7-day purchase rate, cost and margin per Redemption), then per moment the purchase rate with and without an Offer, and Redemption per send against calibration target 4.
- `offer-engine lift fit` learns Estimated Lift for each segment (loyalty tier × days since the last order: ≤30, 31–90, >90 × coupon user, meaning 2+ past Redemptions) and Offer Type. It compares the 7-day purchase rate after the history's random past Offers with the rate at no-offer moments (abandoned carts, 45-day gaps, Friday broadcasts) in the same segment. A segment with fewer than 30 past Offers of a type gets the type's average. It prints the totals and the average per Offer Type. `offer-engine lift show CUSTOMER_ID` prints the Customer's segment at the clock's time and, per Offer Type, the Estimated Lift, the past Offers behind it, both purchase rates and whether it is the segment's own or the average. The fit reads only observable tables, never Ground Truth.
- `offer-engine decisions --last N [--customer ID]` lists Decisions newest first with the Offer's terms, the chooser, the Binding Rule, the rule version and the Validation result.
- Every live Decision keeps a trace of why it came out as it did. `offer-engine decisions explain ID [--prompt]` prints it one stage per block: Trigger, Customer (observable facts only), Checks (opt-out, Holdout, Fatigue Pause, frequency cap, cooldown, eligibility and budget, each passed, stopped or not reached), Eligible Set (each option's ranges, Binding Rules, worst-case cost and Estimated Lift, and the send window), Valuation (the rules chooser's best parameters and value per option), Agent (model, answer, time taken and, with `--prompt`, the full prompt; or why it was skipped), Validation (each check passed or failed, Caption Check included), Fallback (only when the agent's proposal was rejected or its call failed) and Offer. A Decision stopped by a check skips straight to Offer.
- `offer-engine caption-check "TEXT" --offer-type T --param NAME=VALUE --category C` runs the Caption Check and prints `PASS` or one reason per line, exiting 1 on failure. It rejects numbers, ₹ amounts, percentages and multipliers that aren't the Offer's terms (digits, number words, Hinglish, emoji keycaps, Devanagari digits), deadlines ("tonight", "until Sunday", "in 6 hours", "kal tak"), invented urgency ("hurry", "only a few left"), promises the Offer doesn't make ("free gift", "cashback", free delivery on a non-shipping Offer), and captions over 280 characters or blank. Validation runs the same check on every proposed caption.
- `offer-engine personas draft` picks one showcase Customer per case: at-risk high value, new with one order, deal hunter, expiring points, cart abandoner, a Sure Thing, a Holdout Customer and a persuadable one. The criteria are constants in `sim/personas/select.py`, and ties go to the lower ID. Claude (`OFFER_AGENT_MODEL`) then writes a 60–100-word Persona Card for each, with a fictional Indian name. It sees only the profile, the last 180 days of events and Offers, and points, never Ground Truth or the case. Each card goes to `personas/<customer_id>.json` with the case, the generator seed and the population size. A frozen card is never redrafted. `personas show` prints the cards in case order and flags any outside 60–100 words or any missing case. `personas freeze` marks them all frozen after review.
- `offer-engine simulate [--policies …] [--days 28] [--sample 500] [--seed S] [--jobs 4]` compares four Policies over the same simulated days from the clock's current time, each on its own copy of the database, so the live one and its chats are untouched. No Offers always runs as the baseline. Blanket 20% gives 20% off the Customer's top category whenever they hold no live Offer, Rules Only uses the rules chooser, and Rules + Agent uses the agent (`FAST_AGENT_MODEL`) for a 500-Customer sample stratified by segment. It prints one row per Policy for all Customers and for the sample: Offers, Claims, Redemptions, spend, Incremental Margin, Incremental Margin per ₹ of spend, spend on Sure Things, Validation rejections, Fallbacks and runtime, then Estimated Lift against Ground Truth Lift by decile. Each run is saved to `sim_policy_results`. Agent answers are cached, so a rerun from the same state makes no calls.
- `offer-engine demo snapshot` copies the database to `data/demo.db`, and `offer-engine demo reset` copies it back into the open database. The bots and the console keep running and see the snapshot's world on their next query. Both print the simulated time and the Customer count. `--path` picks another snapshot file.
- `AGENT_MODE` in `.env` sets how the offer agent and the Customer Bots call Claude. `live` (the default) calls it. `record` calls it and appends each answer to `demo/agent_recordings.jsonl`. `replay` returns the recorded answer for the same model, system prompt, tools and messages, and needs no API key. A request with no recording calls Claude and records the answer when a key is set. Without a key, the offer agent falls back to the rules chooser and a Customer Bot flips on Ground Truth alone. `DEMO_SEED` derives each Offer code from the seed, the Customer and the Decision time, so a rerun from the snapshot sends the same codes.
- `offer-engine demo list` shows the demo scenarios by group. `demo run NAME` fires the scenario's Trigger for its Customer at the clock's time and prints each trace stage, the Decision and the outcome: Ground Truth's 7-day chance of buying, the Customer Bot's message fit and the chance it gives, a flip seeded by `SEED` and the scenario name, and the Customer Bot's and Offer Bot's replies. The runner marks the Offer sent and settles it, so it never reaches Telegram. A setup that changes a Customer (a `/stop`, three "Not now"s) is undone afterwards, and a rerun first clears that Customer's earlier run, so a rerun prints the same thing. `demo check` runs every scenario on a fresh copy of `data/demo.db`, prints PASS or FAIL for each and exits 1 on any FAIL. The ten scenarios are `sure-thing`, `lost-cause-nudge`, `real-discount`, `holdout`, `opted-out`, `fatigue-pause`, `agent-breaks-rule`, `manipulative-caption`, `rule-change` and `withdraw`. `agent-breaks-rule` changes the agent's answer to 40% off and `manipulative-caption` changes its caption to "Hurry, 30% off tonight only!". In both, Validation rejects the answer and the Fallback's Offer goes out. `rule-change` decides for the `real-discount` Customer, previews and saves a bronze tier maximum of 5% as author `demo`, decides again (7% off becomes 5%) and saves the original Rules back as a new version. `withdraw` queues two Offers for its Customer, whose Decision is then stopped by the frequency cap, and runs the emergency withdraw. `demo run` prints what these steps report after the outcome, with a spinner while it runs.

## Known limits

- Auto mode runs only while the Simulation Clock page is open; switching pages or closing the tab stops it. The one-step-at-a-time guard covers the console, not a `clock advance` run from a terminal at the same time. On 10k Customers a day takes about 15 s, about 11 s of it the Daily Sweep, plus up to 20 s for each agent Decision (1–9 a day with the Customer Bots and a Tester linked). Auto mode's ticks are skipped while a day runs, so days come slower than the slider says.
- `simulate` with all four Policies on 10k Customers takes about 100 minutes (`--jobs 4`), and Rules + Agent makes about 4,500 agent calls on its first run. Results come from one seed, so small gaps between Policies may be noise. Offers still open at the end count as unredeemed.
- A preview runs only the Daily Sweep, on the world as it is now. It skips the events and Trigger Decisions that would come before 10:00, and the agent never decides. Its Offer counts include Nudges. It needs free disk next to the database for two copies (about 1.2 GB on 10k Customers).
- A Rules page open in two tabs keeps the older tab's typed values until it reruns. Saving there still saves a new version on top of the other tab's, so check the history.
- If the Offer Bot is sending a queued Offer's card while a withdraw runs, the card can arrive after the withdraw with no notice. Claiming it gets "That code isn't active any more."
- Only `offer-engine trigger cart-abandoned` fires a Trigger by hand.
- Decisions made before traces were kept, and those made inside `simulate` or Preview impact, have no trace. `decisions explain` says so.
- The rules chooser can offer a different category from the abandoned cart. Until `lift fit` runs, it and the agent use fixed Lift guesses per Offer Type. After it, single segments' estimates are noisy (a standard error of 3–5 points against true Lift of 1–3), so the chooser can pick an Offer Type on noise. `generate` deletes the fit, and nothing refits it on its own.
- The agent decides only for chat-attached Customers, so link one with `chats link` first. Its path has run against the real API once by hand (T10's Check); tests use a fake client.
- Past Offers are redeemed far more often than calibration targets 3 and 4 allow (about 15% of targeted sends against 1.5–5%), because every Customer reads every Offer and every buyer uses the code (docs/QnA.md Q41). Generating 10k Customers × 12 months takes about 80 seconds; one live day for them takes about 15 s with the agent off, 11 s of it the Daily Sweep.
- With the fixed Lift guesses, the Daily Sweep's rules chooser sends a ₹0 Nudge to most Customers it decides for: about 8,000 of 10,000 on the first day. Not re-measured after `lift fit`.
- The Caption Check accepts a caption that names the wrong category, and "today" or "this week" when not worded as a deadline.
- `setup` does not ask for `DB_PATH`, `SEED` or `CONSOLE_PASSWORD`. Edit `.env` by hand to set them.
- A Customer Bot draws its fate when the card arrives. An Offer sent while the Customer Bot process is down never gets one and only expires. Each reply takes about 10 wall-clock seconds. A redemption order never burns points. A Tester who claims and doesn't buy hears nothing.
- A Tester's copy is a snapshot from the first pick. It doesn't follow the original's later events and never browses, carts or orders on its own, so only `/offers` and the Daily Sweep decide for it. An Offer from `/offers` outside the send window (09:00–21:00 by default) waits until the clock reaches the window.
- A send Telegram refuses stays queued and is retried every 5 s until the Offer expires. Queued Offers past expiry are never sent and become `expired`.
- The Persona Cards in `personas/` belong to the world from `generate --customers 10000 --seed 7` at the clock start. On another world `personas draft` stops with an error when a frozen card's Customer isn't picked, and it deletes unfrozen drafts that aren't picked.
- Ctrl-C on `offer-engine start` or `make` can print a `KeyboardInterrupt` traceback. Everything still stops, the Customer Bots a few seconds later.
- The Results page's Policy sections show the latest `simulate` run, which may start from an earlier clock time than the live sections. Its Holdout comparison counts every Customer outside the Holdout, offered or not, and uses today's Holdout percentage, so changing it mid-run moves Customers between the groups.
- `demo reset` on the 10k world takes about 12 s and `demo snapshot` about 5 s. A recording matches only the exact prompt, so a changed Rule, clock time or Customer history misses and calls Claude (or falls back without a key).
- `demo check` copies the 593 MB snapshot once per scenario into `data/` and takes about 2 minutes. `rule-change` alone takes about 60 s, as it runs Preview impact on 10k Customers. The scenarios expect the snapshot's clock time (2026-12-01 00:00); after the clock moves, `demo run` may FAIL until `demo reset`. Scenario Offers get seeded codes, so their Customer Bot answers replay; changing a scenario's Customer, Trigger or the Rules needs new recordings. `AGENT_MODE=replay offer-engine demo check` with a key records only the missing answers; `AGENT_MODE=record` asks Claude again for every scenario and may change answers the other scenarios expect.
- `withdraw` withdraws every outstanding live Offer, not only its own. After a Daily Sweep in a live demo it pulls the sweep's Offers too. Each `rule-change` run adds two Rule versions by `demo` to the history.
